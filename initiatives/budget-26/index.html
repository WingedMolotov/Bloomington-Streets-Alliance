2027-2028 City Budget

Bloomington's municipal budget determines how your tax dollars are allocated across city services. New this year, the budget be on a two year cycle. This means that what the city council decides this year will determine spending for the next two years (2027 and 2028).

Budget engagment has already begun, and will continue through the rest of the year. Here's the timeline:

  • August 17, 2026: City Council holds its first study session on the budget and tax levy
    • In-person public comment opportunity
    • Let's Talk Bloomington comments are shared with City Council
  • September 14, 2026: City Council sets the preliminary tax levy
    • NEW: In-person public comment opportunity
  • November 16, 2026: City Council holds its second study session on the budget and tax levy
    • In-person public comment opportunity
    • Let's Talk Bloomington comments are shared with City Council
  • December 7, 2026: City Council holds a Truth-in-Taxation public hearing on the budget and tax levy, as mandated by state law
    • Opportunity to speak in-person at public hearing
    • Let's Talk Bloomington comments are shared with City Council
  • December 14, 2026: City Council adopts the final budget and tax levy

Realize the bold visions the city has already established in the the Active Transportation Action Plan, the Neighborhood Traffic Management Plan, and the Bloomington Ferry Road and American Boulevard studies. Use this to not only reduce the heat island and auto emissions from asphalt, but cut down on repaving $$$. Provide local funding matches for state and county roadway projects to leverage the money these agencies are spending toward the benefit of active transportation in Bloomington. Give Metro Transit a reason to expand service in the city. Connect the investments in schools, parks, community centers, and neighborhood commercial centers through an All Ages and Abilities (AAA) bike network."

"Right-sizing roadways whenever possible to save costs on road construction and maintenance- roadways like Collegeview, Old Shakopee in South Loop, 30th Ave, Bloomington Ferry, Auto Club Rd, Xerxes Ave S, that were build significantly wider than current road volumes warrant, especially roads like Bloomington Ferry that were built to handle traffic pre-169 that have way lower volumes. Our commercial corridors are significant to our city, and retail workers that are the backbone of the Bloomington economy need housing that fits our needs. Higher density, mixed-use on corridors like Lyndale place people close to where they work, shop, entertain, and go to school."
